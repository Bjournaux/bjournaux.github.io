# Research

The primary research goal of our group is to identify and characterize the chemical and physical properties of extraterrestrial water-rich environments in icy moons and water-rich exoplanets, and to determine the limiting factors for the habitability of deep extraterrestrial oceans.
We take part in preparing upcoming space mission observations of icy moons (Europa Clipper, Dragonfly, JUICE).
We focus on the main research topics described below.

## Water-rich mineral physics, thermodynamics and petrology {#mineral-physics}

Our lab studies water-rich mineral phases such as the many water ice polymorphs and the hydrates of salts and volatile species (e.g. clathrates).
We use high-pressure apparatus like [diamond anvil cells](https://serc.carleton.edu/NAGTWorkshops/mineralogy/mineral_physics/diamond_anvil.html), combined with [THz Raman spectroscopy](https://www.coherent.com/web-resources/white-paper/intro-to-thz-raman-spectroscopy-wp) and [synchrotron X-ray diffraction](https://serc.carleton.edu/NAGTWorkshops/mineralogy/mineral_physics/synchrotron_xrd.html), among others, to characterize crystalline phases and their thermodynamic properties (density, stability, thermophysical properties).
Many of these phases are expected inside and at the surface of icy moons and exoplanets, where they affect geodynamics and long-term evolution.
Because of the unique conditions found in these planetary bodies, we are discovering new crystalline species, such as [three new NaCl hydrates, reported in PNAS in 2023](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2217125120).

## Thermodynamics and physical properties of aqueous systems at extreme conditions {#thermodynamics}

Our group has unique expertise in measuring ultrasonic sound speeds in aqueous solutions, densities of solids, and thermodynamic equilibria at elevated pressures.
From these measurements we derive fundamental thermodynamic representations of liquids and solids.
These equations of state are compiled and implemented in our open-source code [SeaFreeze](../seafreeze), which is regularly updated with new representations.
We are also interested in the transport properties of these materials at extreme conditions, such as electrical conductivity and rheology.

## Icy moons and exoplanets geophysical modeling {#geophysical-modeling}

All the experimental data gathered by our group are used directly in planetary geophysical modeling to constrain the interior structures of icy moons and exoplanets.
This is done in collaboration with NASA JPL through the [PlanetProfile](https://github.com/vancesteven/PlanetProfile) project, and with other groups who implement SeaFreeze in their geophysical frameworks.
This has enabled significant advances in modeling icy moons such as Titan, its [geodynamic evolution](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2021GL097602) and [seismic response](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/PSJ/ac787e), as well as [exoplanet habitability](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-35187-4), among other examples.

## Planetary exploration and cryobiology {#applications}

Thermodynamic results from our group are used to prepare planetary mission observations.
Sound speed and seismic velocity data are used for the seismometer of the DraGMet instrument suite on NASA's [Dragonfly mission](https://dragonfly.jhuapl.edu/).
Density and crystallography data support the gravimetry and near-infrared surface spectroscopy of NASA's Europa Clipper and ESA's JUICE missions.
Our data also support the development of transformative organ cryopreservation technologies, in collaboration with the [Public Thermo Lab at Texas A&M](https://publicthermo.com/).

## Other research topics {#other}

Our group is also interested in the mechanical deformation of cryomaterials for glaciology and planetary science, in particular the application of EBSD methods to icy materials.
We also develop THz Raman spectroscopy techniques for the geosciences and planetary sciences.
