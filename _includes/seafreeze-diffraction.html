How it is computed

Phase diagram. See the SeaFreeze visualizer. Open the phase diagram on its own page.

Metastable ices. At each point, every ice that SeaFreeze evaluates there is listed, with its Gibbs energy above the stable phase, ΔG = G − Gstable. Away from their stability fields the SeaFreeze representations are extrapolations, more uncertain the larger ΔG. The density of a metastable ice comes from SeaFreeze on the same grid as the diagram (every second node, density within 3 × 10−6).

Lattice parameters. SeaFreeze gives the density ρ of each phase at the chosen pressure and temperature. The unit-cell volume is V = Z M / (ρ NA), with M the molar mass of H2O and Z the number of molecules in the cell. The reference cell of each ice is then scaled isotropically to that volume: its axial ratios (c/a, b/a) and angles are kept, so compression is assumed to be the same along every axis. The volume and density are exact (within SeaFreeze's accuracy); individual axes can differ from measurements where compression is anisotropic.

Intensities. Reflections come from the reference structures below (average structures with partial hydrogen occupancies for the proton-disordered ices). The fractional coordinates are kept fixed. Each peak's intensity is m |F|² times a geometric factor. For X-rays this is the Lorentz–polarization factor of an unpolarized beam, (1 + cos² 2θ) / (sin² θ cos θ), with O and H atomic form factors (International Tables, Cromer–Mann). For neutrons it is d⁴ against d-spacing (time of flight) and 1 / (sin² θ cos θ) against 2θ (constant wavelength), with coherent scattering lengths bO = 5.803, bD = 6.671 and bH = −3.739 fm. Isotropic displacement parameters UO = 0.020 Å² and UH = 0.035 Å² are used for every phase. Peaks are Gaussians of constant Δd/d, and each phase is scaled to its own strongest peak. H2O also gives a large incoherent neutron background, which is not shown. Liquid water and vapour have no Bragg peaks.
