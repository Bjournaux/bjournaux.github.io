Our new open-access paper in *Earth and Planetary Science Letters*, led by Anna Pakhomova (European Synchrotron Radiation Facility), shows that high pressure helps methanol become trapped in clathrate hydrates, ice-like crystals that cage small molecules, deep inside large ocean worlds.

**Synchrotron experiments.** We grew methanol-bearing clathrate crystals in diamond anvil cells at high pressure (up to about 1.7 GPa) and low temperature, and solved their structures *in situ* by single-crystal X-ray diffraction on beamline ID15B of the [ESRF synchrotron](https://www.esrf.fr/) in Grenoble, France. The intense, finely focused synchrotron X-ray beam makes it possible to identify these tiny crystals as they form inside the diamond anvil cell.

**What it means for Titan.**

- Pressure is needed to trap methanol in clathrates, so only large icy bodies can store it deep inside. This may explain why methanol is seen on comets and small icy bodies, but not on the surface of large ones such as Titan.
- Up to 4 wt % of Titan's primordial hydrosphere could have been methanol, stored as clathrate in its high-pressure ice layer. Hydrothermal activity at the base of this layer could release it and convert it into methane by hydrogenolysis.
- This deep reservoir could replenish the methane that Titan's atmosphere loses to photochemistry for about 160 billion years at the current rate, a new explanation for how Titan keeps producing methane today.
