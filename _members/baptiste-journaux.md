Baptiste Journaux is an Assistant Professor in the Department of Earth and Space Sciences and a member of the Astrobiology faculty at the University of Washington.
He is a planetary scientist and mineral physicist with a background in geophysics and glaciology, who studies high-pressure aqueous systems, and the properties of salt–organic–water crystalline solids and liquids at conditions relevant to the deep oceans of icy moons and exoplanets.

He is the main developer of the [SeaFreeze](../../seafreeze) package, which provides an accurate representation of the thermodynamics of liquid water and high-pressure ices.
The primary research goal of his group is to identify and characterize the chemical and physical limiting factors for habitability, and to identify geophysical signatures of deep extraterrestrial oceans on icy moons and water-rich exoplanets.
He is a member of the [NASA Dragonfly mission](https://dragonfly.jhuapl.edu/) [science team](https://dragonfly.jhuapl.edu/Our-Team/) and a University of Washington expert for space and aeronautics.

## Research interests

Planetary science, experimental high-pressure mineral physics, astrobiology, thermodynamic and mechanical properties of water-rich minerals, petrology, interior structure and dynamic evolution of planetary bodies, and robotic space exploration.
